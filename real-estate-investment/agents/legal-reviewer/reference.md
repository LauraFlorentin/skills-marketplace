# Skill 10: Legal Document Red Flag Reviewer

Analyze investment legal documents (shareholders agreements, PPMs, operating agreements, subscription agreements) to identify investor-unfriendly terms, missing protections, and contractual red flags. This skill does NOT provide legal advice — it flags issues for the investor to discuss with their attorney.

**Always caveat**: "This analysis identifies potential concerns in the document structure. Consult a qualified attorney in the relevant jurisdiction before making investment decisions."

## When to Use

- User uploads or shares a shareholders agreement, PPM, operating agreement, or subscription document
- User asks "are the terms fair?" or "what should I watch out for?"
- User is reviewing legal documents before signing an investment commitment
- As part of a full deal analysis when legal documents are available

## Document Review Checklist

### Category 1: Investor Economic Rights

```
ECONOMIC RIGHTS REVIEW
  □ Dividend/Distribution Rights
    - Are distributions mandatory or discretionary?
    - What triggers a distribution? (quarterly, annually, at GP discretion)
    - What is the distribution priority/waterfall?
    - Is there a preferred return? What rate?
    - Are unpaid distributions cumulative or non-cumulative?
    - Can distributions be suspended? Under what conditions?
    
  □ Liquidation Preference
    - Do investors get their money back before GP/founder?
    - Is it a 1x preference (standard) or more/less?
    - What is the payment order? (creditors → investors → GP)
    
  □ Anti-Dilution
    - Can the company issue more shares and dilute existing investors?
    - Is investor consent required for new issuances?
    - Do existing investors have preemptive rights?
    
  □ Fee Transparency
    - Are all fees clearly defined with exact percentages?
    - What is each fee calculated on? (gross, net, NAV, etc.)
    - Are there any hidden fees (transaction fees, admin fees)?
    - Can fees be changed without investor consent?
```

**Red flags:**
- Distributions are entirely at GP discretion with no defined schedule
- Non-cumulative preferred return (missed payments are lost forever)
- No liquidation preference (investor and GP share losses equally from dollar one)
- Company can issue unlimited new shares without investor consent
- Fees can be modified by GP/management unilaterally

### Category 2: Governance & Control Rights

```
GOVERNANCE REVIEW
  □ Voting Rights
    - What can investors vote on?
    - What decisions require investor consent?
    - What decisions can GP make unilaterally?
    
  □ Major Decision Consent
    These decisions should REQUIRE investor consent:
    - Sale of the property or company
    - Refinancing or taking on new debt
    - Major capital expenditures (above a threshold)
    - Changes to the business plan
    - Related-party transactions
    - Amendments to the agreement itself
    - Admission of new investors
    - Dissolution/liquidation
    
  □ Information Rights
    - Frequency of financial reporting (quarterly minimum)
    - Audit requirement (annual independent audit)
    - Access to books and records
    - Right to inspect the property
    
  □ GP Removal
    - Can investors remove an underperforming GP/manager?
    - What threshold is needed? (majority, supermajority)
    - What constitutes "cause" for removal?
    - Is there a "no-fault" removal mechanism?
```

**Red flags:**
- Investors have NO consent rights on ANY major decisions
- GP can sell the property without investor approval
- GP can take on unlimited debt
- No audit requirement or reporting obligation
- No mechanism to remove underperforming GP
- GP can amend the agreement without investor consent

### Category 3: Transfer & Exit Rights

```
TRANSFER & EXIT REVIEW
  □ Transfer Restrictions
    - Can investors sell/transfer their interest?
    - Is GP/company consent required? Can it be unreasonably withheld?
    - Right of First Refusal (ROFR) — who has it? How long?
    - Transfer fees — how much?
    - Minimum transfer units (can you sell partial interests?)
    
  □ Lock-Up Period
    - How long before transfers are permitted?
    - Is there a penalty for early exit?
    
  □ Forced Sale / Drag-Along
    - Can GP force investors to sell?
    - Under what conditions?
    - Is the price fair market value or at GP's determination?
    
  □ Forced Buyback / Repurchase
    - Can the company buy back investor shares involuntarily?
    - What triggers forced repurchase?
    - How is the repurchase price determined?
    - Is there independent valuation?
    
  □ Tag-Along Rights
    - If GP sells their interest, can investors sell alongside?
    - On the same terms?
```

**Red flags:**
- Transfer requires GP consent that can be withheld at GP's "sole discretion"
- Lock-up period > 5 years with no liquidity mechanism
- GP can force buyback at a formula price (not fair market value)
- Forced repurchase triggered by vague terms ("reputational harm," "obstruction")
- No tag-along rights (GP can sell and leave investors stuck)
- Excessive transfer fees (>2% is aggressive)
- Fractional transfers prohibited (must sell all or nothing)

### Category 4: Capital Commitments & Default

```
CAPITAL COMMITMENT REVIEW
  □ Call Structure
    - Total commitment amount
    - Call schedule and amounts
    - Notice period for capital calls (minimum 10-14 business days standard)
    - Maximum number of calls
    
  □ Default Consequences
    - What happens if investor misses a capital call?
    - Industry standard: 10-25% dilution penalty
    - Acceptable: Forced sale of interest at discount (10-20%)
    - Aggressive: 50%+ forfeiture of prior contributions
    - Unacceptable: Total forfeiture of all prior contributions
    
  □ Over-Call Protection
    - Is there a cap on total capital calls?
    - Can GP call more than the committed amount?
```

**Red flags:**
- Default penalty > 25% of contributed capital (punitive)
- Forfeiture of 50%+ of contributions for a single missed call
- Short notice period (<10 business days) for capital calls
- No cap on total capital that can be called
- Additional capital calls beyond committed amount without consent

### Category 5: Conflict of Interest Protections

```
CONFLICT OF INTEREST REVIEW
  □ Related-Party Transactions
    - Can GP/manager transact with the company?
    - Are related-party transactions disclosed?
    - Do they require independent approval?
    - Is there a fair market value requirement?
    
  □ GP Other Activities
    - Can GP operate competing investments?
    - Non-compete provisions?
    - Duty of loyalty / fiduciary duty provisions?
    
  □ Key Person Provisions
    - What happens if the founder/key person departs?
    - Suspension of new investments?
    - Investor right to exit?
```

**Red flags:**
- GP can self-deal without disclosure or approval
- Management company is owned by GP (double-dipping on fees)
- No restrictions on GP operating competing investments
- No key-person clause (if founder leaves, nothing changes)
- Payments flow through GP-affiliated entities before reaching investors

### Category 6: Protective Clauses

```
PROTECTIVE CLAUSE CHECKLIST
  □ Cooling-Off Period
    - Duration: [X days — 14 days is standard in EU/UK]
    - Full refund within cooling-off? [Yes/No]
    
  □ Force Majeure
    - Clearly defined? [Yes/No]
    - Reasonable scope? [Yes/No]
    - Does it suspend distributions indefinitely? [Concerning if yes]
    - Recovery timeline requirements? [Yes/No]
    
  □ Dispute Resolution
    - Mechanism: [Negotiation → Mediation → Arbitration → Litigation]
    - Arbitration seat and rules: [Where, which institution]
    - Language: [Investor's language?]
    - Cost allocation: [Each party bears own / Loser pays]
    
  □ Limited Liability
    - Investor liability limited to committed capital? [Yes/No]
    - No personal guarantees required? [Confirm]
    - Indemnification provisions? [Who indemnifies whom]
    
  □ Insurance Requirements
    - Property insurance required? [Yes/No]
    - Liability insurance? [Yes/No]
    - Key-person insurance? [Yes/No]
    - D&O insurance? [Yes/No]
```
