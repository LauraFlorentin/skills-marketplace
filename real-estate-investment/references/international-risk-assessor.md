# Skill 8: International & Cross-Border Risk Assessor

Evaluate risks specific to international real estate investments including jurisdiction risk, currency exposure, foreign ownership structures, political/regulatory risk, and cross-border tax implications. International deals carry an entire layer of risk that domestic investments don't — this skill systematically identifies and quantifies those risks.

## When to Use

- Investment is in a foreign country or involves cross-border entities
- Deal uses offshore SPVs or multi-jurisdiction holding structures
- Land tenure is leasehold (not freehold) or involves foreign ownership restrictions
- Currency of investment differs from investor's home currency
- Emerging market investment (Southeast Asia, Latin America, Africa, Eastern Europe)
- User asks about investing in Bali, Thailand, Mexico, Dubai, Portugal, etc.

### Country Profiles: Ownership Structures

#### Indonesia / Bali

Indonesian law reserves freehold (Hak Milik) exclusively for Indonesian citizens. Foreigners must use one of these structures:

| Title Type | Definition | Term | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hak Pakai | Right to Use | 25 years, extendable to 70 total | Long-term residential use (requires KITAS/KITAP permit) |
| HGB (Hak Guna Bangunan) | Right to Build | 30 years initial, extendable + renewable to 80 total | Commercial investment via PT PMA — most secure for investors |
| Hak Sewa | Leasehold/Rental | 25-50 years (contractual) | Straightforward lease with local landowner |

**PT PMA (Foreign Investment Company)**: The most secure structure for serious investors. A PT PMA is an Indonesian legal entity that can hold HGB title, operate rental businesses legally, and access corporate financing. Requirements (as of BKPM Reg 5/2025): minimum total investment plan of IDR 10 billion (~$600K USD) per KBLI, paid-up capital of IDR 2.5 billion (~$150K USD). The PT PMA structure bypasses illegal "nominee" arrangements that have historically led to total investment losses.

**Active-Use Enforcement (PP 18/2021)**: The government can revoke HGB titles if land remains "idle" for more than 2 years. Investors must demonstrate active use (construction, leasing, business operations) within the timeframe or risk the property reverting to state land (tanah negara). This is strictly enforced as of 2025-2026 under Governor Koster's permit crackdowns.

**SLF Certificate**: Sertifikat Laik Fungsi (Certificate of Occupational Fitness) is required for luxury villas and hospitality properties. Verify this exists or can be obtained before investing.

**Red flags specific to Bali:**
- Property marketed with "nominee" ownership structure (illegal, unenforceable)
- No IMB/PBG building permit or SLF occupational certificate
- Leasehold on unregistered land (not filed with BPN land office)
- HGB held by dormant company without active-use compliance
- Zoning does not permit "Commercial Rental" use for STR platforms

#### Mexico / Tulum

The Mexican Constitution prohibits foreigners from direct ownership within the "Restricted Zone" (within 50km of coast or 100km of border). This includes all of Tulum, Cancun, and most desirable coastal markets.

**Fideicomiso (Bank Trust)**: A Mexican bank holds legal title as trustee; the foreign buyer is the beneficiary with full rights to use, rent, sell, and inherit. Valid for 50 years and renewable in perpetuity.

| Transaction Cost | Tulum 2026 Estimate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ISABI (Acquisition Tax) | ~3.0% | Varies by municipality |
| Fideicomiso Setup | $1,500-$2,500 USD | One-time trust creation + SRE permit |
| Annual Trust Fee | $500-$1,000 USD | Ongoing bank maintenance |
| Notary & Registration | 1.5-2.5% | Deed formalization + public registry |

**CRITICAL: Ejido Land Risk**: Ejido is communal agrarian property that CANNOT be legally sold to outsiders unless fully regularized through a formal government process. Ejido fraud is the #1 cause of investment loss in Tulum — reports indicate 7 out of 10 purchasers encounter some form of fraudulent activity involving Ejido land. Always verify: obtain a Certificado de Libertad de Gravamen (No-Lien Certificate) from the Public Registry and confirm the land is NOT Ejido or has completed full regularization.

**Red flags specific to Mexico/Tulum:**
- Property on Ejido or previously-Ejido land without completed regularization
- No Fideicomiso in place for coastal property (direct foreign ownership is illegal)
- Developer selling pre-construction without proper permits
- Unusually low price compared to neighboring properties (title defect indicator)
- Seller unable to produce No-Lien Certificate
- La Veleta / Aldea Zama oversupply (prices down 30% from speculative peaks)

### 2. Land Tenure & Leasehold Risk

For many international markets, foreigners cannot own freehold land. Leasehold creates unique risks:

```
LEASEHOLD ANALYSIS
  Lease Type:               [Hak Sewa / Hak Pakai / Hak Guna Bangunan / etc.]
  Initial Term:             X years
  Extension Rights:         [Contractual / Statutory / Uncertain]
  Years Remaining:          X years
  Extension Cost:           [Known / Unknown / To be negotiated]
  
  CRITICAL QUESTIONS:
  - Is the lease registered with the land office / government?
  - Who is the landowner? (Individual, government, community)
  - Can the landowner sell to someone else during the lease?
  - What happens to improvements at lease end? (Revert to owner?)
  - Are extension rights contractual (enforceable) or merely indicative?
  - Has the landowner historically honored extensions?
  
  LEASEHOLD VALUATION IMPACT:
    Years Remaining    Value Impact
    40-50 years        Minimal discount (~5-10%)
    25-40 years        Moderate discount (10-25%)
    15-25 years        Significant discount (25-40%)
    <15 years          Severe discount (40-60%+)
    
  This deal: X years remaining → X% leasehold discount factor
  
  DEPRECIATION TO ZERO:
    Unlike freehold, leasehold assets approach zero value at expiry.
    Investor must recover full capital + returns within the lease term.
    Required annual yield to recover capital:
      = Investment ÷ (Remaining Years × Expected Annual Net Income)
```

**Red flags:**
- Lease < 25 years remaining with no guaranteed extension
- Extension rights are "best efforts" or "subject to negotiation" (not guaranteed)
- Landowner is an individual (harder to enforce vs. government/institution)
- Improvements revert to landowner at lease end
- Lease not registered with government land office
- No clear mechanism for lease extension cost determination

### 3. Currency & FX Risk

```
CURRENCY RISK ANALYSIS
  Investment Currency:      [EUR / USD / etc.]
  Operating Currency:       [local currency]
  Revenue Currency:         [local / mixed / USD-linked]
  Repatriation Currency:    [investor's home currency]
  
  HISTORICAL VOLATILITY:
    [Local currency] vs [Investment currency]:
    1-year range:    X% depreciation to X% appreciation
    5-year trend:    X% total movement
    10-year trend:   X% total movement
    
  IMPACT SCENARIOS:
    Base case (flat FX):        X% return in home currency
    Local currency -10%:        X% return (adjusted)
    Local currency -20%:        X% return (adjusted)
    Local currency -30%:        X% return (adjusted)
    
  NATURAL HEDGES:
    - Revenue in USD/EUR (e.g., tourist payments in hard currency)? [Yes/No]
    - Pricing indexed to USD/EUR? [Yes/No]
    - Operating costs in local currency while revenue in hard currency? [Yes/No]
    
  FX RISK RATING: [Low / Moderate / High / Very High]
```

**Three Types of Currency Risk:**
- **Transaction Risk**: Exchange rate shifts between deal agreement and final payment. Especially prevalent in multi-stage transactions or pre-construction purchases where capital is deployed over months/years.
- **Translation Risk**: Accounting risk where the value of a foreign asset fluctuates on the investor's home balance sheet due to currency movements, regardless of local performance.
- **Economic Risk (Forecast Risk)**: Long-term currency movements driven by inflation differentials, interest rate policy, and monetary policy. A weakening local currency erodes hard-currency returns over a multi-year hold. This is the primary risk for buy-and-hold international investors.

**Hedging Tools:**
- **Forward contracts**: Lock in exchange rates for future payments (construction installments, repatriation). Available through banks and FX brokers. Cost depends on interest rate differential between currencies.
- **Currency diversification**: Hold assets across regions with uncorrelated currencies to reduce portfolio-level FX impact.
- **USD-denominated pricing**: Price rental revenue in USD/EUR where market permits (common in luxury tourism). Provides natural hedge but shifts FX risk to tenants/guests.
- **Local currency debt**: If financing in local currency, debt service depreciates alongside revenue, creating a natural balance. However, local rates are typically higher (Indonesia: 8-12% for property loans).
- **Revenue timing**: Accelerate profit repatriation during favorable FX windows rather than accumulating local currency reserves.

**Key considerations:**
- Emerging market currencies can lose 20-50% in crisis periods
- IDR has depreciated ~29% vs EUR over 10 years; MXN has been more stable but subject to sudden moves (15-20% in crisis years)
- Revenue in tourism is often partially USD/EUR-denominated (natural hedge)
- Operating costs are almost always in local currency (favorable if local depreciates)
- Repatriation restrictions may prevent moving profits out of country
- Withholding taxes on dividend repatriation vary by country and treaty

### Country-Specific Tax Reference Data

#### Indonesia Tax Benchmarks
| Tax Type | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate Income Tax (PT PMA) | 22% | Standard rate; may be reduced for qualifying SMEs |
| Withholding Tax on Dividends (Art. 26) | 20% standard | Reducible to 5-15% via DTAA with Certificate of Domicile |
| VAT on services | 11% | Applies to hotel/accommodation services |
| Tourism/hotel tax | 10% (varies) | Local government levy |
| Capital gains tax | 22% (corporate) | Part of corporate income tax |
| Property transfer tax (BPHTB) | 5% | Buyer pays on acquisition |
| Luxury goods tax | 20% | On certain high-end property transfers |

**Repatriation process**: Profits from PT PMA must be declared as dividends after annual audit and allocation of mandatory reserves. Requires valid Certificate of Domicile (COD) from investor's home tax authority to claim treaty rates. The 2020 Omnibus Law simplified dividend distribution and introduced incentives for reinvesting profits into Indonesian government bonds or real sector investments.

**DTAAs**: Indonesia has Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements with 70+ countries. Treaty WHT rates on dividends: Netherlands 10%, Singapore 10%, UAE 10%, UK 10%, US 10%, Australia 15%. Always verify current treaty status.

#### Mexico Tax Benchmarks
| Tax Type | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lodging Tax (Impuesto al Hospedaje) | 6.0% | Collected/remitted by platforms like Airbnb |
| Federal Income Tax (ISR) — non-resident | 25% gross OR 35% net | 35% net preferred if costs are documented via legal representative |
| Capital Gains — non-resident | 25% gross or 35% net | Mexico has primary taxing right under Art. 13 of US-Mexico treaty |
| Annual Property Tax (Predial) | 3,000-15,000 MXN | Paid locally to municipality |
| ISABI (Acquisition Tax) | ~3.0% | Varies by municipality; Tulum is higher than Merida |

**US Investor repatriation**: Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) via Form 1116 credits Mexican taxes against US capital gains liability, avoiding double taxation. FBAR and FATCA reporting required for foreign accounts >$10,000.

**2026 Mexico incentive**: Federal Revenue Law offers preferential 15% ISR rate for returning funds held abroad, provided resources are invested in Mexico for minimum 3 years.

**STR Compliance (Quintana Roo)**: Hosts must register with State Tourism Registry (Retur-Q) and comply with lodging tax requirements. Municipalities now have authority to regulate or prohibit short-term rentals under the reformed Tourism Law (effective late 2025).
