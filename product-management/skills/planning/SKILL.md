# Product Planning

Planning artifacts are living decision records. Keep problem, evidence, outcome, scope, and acceptance criteria distinct from a proposed implementation.

## Route

- **Roadmap:** organize outcomes, bets, evidence, dependencies, confidence, and review points. Use dates only when commitments or dependencies justify them.
- **PRD:** define problem, users, evidence, goals, non-goals, requirements, flows, metrics, risks, rollout, and open decisions.
- **Feature specification:** add states, edge cases, permissions, data, accessibility, observability, and acceptance criteria.
- **Prioritization:** choose criteria that reflect the strategy; show raw inputs and sensitivity instead of treating a formula as truth.
- **Backlog refinement:** split by user-visible value, clarify dependencies, add acceptance criteria, and retain unresolved questions.

Read [planning-reference.md](references/planning-reference.md) only when detailed templates or framework mechanics are needed.

## Workflow

1. Establish product context, decision, horizon, constraints, and source evidence.
2. Select the lightest artifact that will unblock the decision.
3. Separate user need and outcome from solution detail.
4. Surface dependencies, opportunity cost, risks, and non-goals.
5. Define observable acceptance criteria and measurement.
6. Identify owners, review cadence, and evidence that should cause revision.

## Quality rules

- Never invent research, effort, confidence, reach, or revenue inputs for a prioritization score.
- Reconcile requirements with privacy, security, accessibility, reliability, legal, and operational needs.
- Avoid commitments disguised as “roadmap themes” and forecasts disguised as facts.
- Preserve dissent and unresolved decisions; do not imply approval that has not occurred.

Return the requested artifact, decision log, assumptions, exclusions, dependencies, risks, and next validation or approval step.
