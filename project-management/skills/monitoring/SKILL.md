# Project Monitoring

Report observed state and uncertainty, not optimism. Establish the reporting cutoff and source for every material status, cost, schedule, scope, quality, benefit, and risk claim.

## Route

- **Status report:** outcomes, milestones, accomplishments, next period, RAG with defined criteria, variances, risks, decisions, and actions.
- **Burndown or forecast:** baseline, completed work, remaining work, throughput or estimate method, scope changes, confidence range, and assumptions.
- **Risk review:** changes in exposure, triggered risks, response effectiveness, new risks, contingencies, and owner actions.
- **Dependency tracker:** provider, consumer, deliverable, need date, confidence, status, impact, escalation, and evidence.
- **Health check:** scope, schedule, cost, quality, team, stakeholder, risk, dependency, and benefit dimensions using explicit evidence.

Read [monitoring-reference.md](references/monitoring-reference.md) only for detailed formulas, templates, and examples.

## Workflow

1. Define period, baseline, thresholds, and audience.
2. Reconcile data across the actual task, finance, quality, and risk systems.
3. Calculate variances with consistent units and show the formula.
4. Separate lagging results, leading indicators, and forecasts.
5. Explain root causes, impact, owner, recovery action, and decision needed.
6. Record data gaps and confidence; never fill them with invented status.

## Quality checks

- RAG ratings use a declared scale and evidence.
- Forecasts show scope change and uncertainty, not one false-precision date.
- Earned-value measures use an approved baseline and valid actual-cost data.
- Team health avoids individual surveillance or unsupported performance inference.
- Reports preserve bad news and dissent rather than smoothing them away.

Return the report, calculation evidence, decisions required, action owners, and next measurement date.
