# Project Closing

Close evidence and obligations, not just tasks. Confirm acceptance, ownership, operations readiness, finance, contracts, data retention, and unresolved risk with accountable stakeholders.

## Route

- **Retrospective:** choose a psychologically safe format, collect observations, identify systemic causes, prioritize improvements, and assign a small number of owned experiments.
- **Lessons learned:** record context, evidence, outcome, contributing factors, recommendation, applicability, owner, and retrieval tags.
- **Closeout:** verify deliverables and acceptance, open items, transition, support, financial and contract closure, benefits ownership, records, access, assets, compliance, and approvals.

Read [closing-reference.md](references/closing-reference.md) only for detailed facilitation formats, checklists, and examples.

## Workflow

1. Establish closure type, criteria, authority, and effective date.
2. Reconcile plan, deliverables, acceptance evidence, decisions, changes, risks, costs, and benefits.
3. Surface open work and transfer each item to a named owner with due date and escalation path.
4. Facilitate learning without blame or unsupported individual-performance claims.
5. Confirm operational, security, privacy, legal, financial, vendor, and records obligations as relevant.
6. Record approval and archive location; do not claim closure before acceptance.

## Quality checks

- Lessons are specific, evidence-based, and reusable.
- Actions have owners and review dates.
- Access removal, deletion, and archival follow policy and authorization.
- Benefits that continue after closure have an owner and measurement plan.
- Outstanding defects, risks, debt, and disputes remain visible.

Return the requested artifact, acceptance and approval status, open-item handoff, lessons, and post-close review date.
