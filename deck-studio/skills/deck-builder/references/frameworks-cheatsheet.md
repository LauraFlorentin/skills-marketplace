# Frameworks Cheatsheet

Quick-reference for Stage 1 framework selection.

---

## Pyramid Principle
**Use for:** Any exec briefing, email recommendation, individual argument slide
**Structure:** Answer → 3–5 Key Arguments → Evidence
**Key rule:** Lead with the conclusion. Always.

---

## SCQA (Situation–Complication–Question–Answer)
**Use for:** Executive summary slide, meeting openers, email subject lines
**Structure:**
- Situation: Undisputed, shared context (1–2 sentences)
- Complication: The tension or disruption
- Question: Implicit ("So what do we do?")
- Answer: Your governing thought / recommendation

---

## SCR (Situation–Complication–Resolution)
**Use for:** Full consulting deck architecture
**Structure:**
- Situation (1–3 slides): Shared ground truth
- Complication (1–3 slides): The problem/opportunity — keep short
- Resolution (60–80% of deck): Your answer, structured by Pyramid Principle

---

## Gap Framework — "What Is / What Could Be" (Nancy Duarte)
**Use for:** Resistant or complacent audiences; transformation pitches
**Structure:**
1. Vividly paint "What Is" — current pain, risk, inefficiency
2. Contrast with "What Could Be" — specific aspirational future
3. Identify the gap — root causes
4. The Bridge — your recommended strategy to close it

---

## Hero's Journey (Consulting Variant)
**Use for:** Change management, organizational transformation, skeptical audiences
**Structure:**
1. The World Before (empathy for current state)
2. The Call to Change (the disruption)
3. The Challenge (acknowledge difficulty)
4. The Path (your recommended solution)
5. The World After (vision of success)
6. The Ask

---

## Data Story Arc (Brent Dykes)
**Use for:** Data-heavy presentations where the insight needs to land
**Structure:**
1. Hook — striking data point or question
2. Context — background to interpret the data
3. Conflict — the insight that disrupts expectations
4. Aha Moment — the thing that explains the conflict
5. Implications — the "so what"
6. Call to Action — what you need from the audience

---

## MECE Check (applies to all frameworks)
Arguments at any level must be:
- **Mutually Exclusive:** No overlap between points
- **Collectively Exhaustive:** Together they cover the full argument, no gaps

Run this check on every set of 3–5 bullets, arguments, or chapters before finalizing.
