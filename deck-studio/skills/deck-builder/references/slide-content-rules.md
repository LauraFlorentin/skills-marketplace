# Slide Content Rules

Rules and templates for writing high-quality slide content in Stage 3.

---

## The Non-Negotiables

1. **One idea per slide.** If you say "and also" when describing a slide, split it.
2. **Action titles only.** Every title is a complete sentence that makes a claim.
3. **3 bullets max** for argument slides. More = you haven't synthesized enough.
4. **Bullets are insights, not topics.** Every bullet should contain a specific claim or data point.
5. **Every chart has an action title** — not a metric label.
6. **"So what?" test:** If you can't answer it for a title or bullet, rewrite it.

---

## Action Title Formula

`[Subject] + [Verb] + [Specific insight or implication]`

| ❌ Topic Title | ✅ Action Title |
|---------------|----------------|
| Market Overview | Digital challenger banks captured 18% of new accounts in 24 months |
| Cost Analysis | Three initiatives can reduce operating costs by $85M over 18 months |
| Revenue Update | Revenue declined 12% YoY — volume loss in digital channels is the driver |
| AI Strategy | Accelerating AI deployment across three fronts can restore $40M in margin |
| Customer Data | NPS dropped 14 points — digital experience failures are the root cause |

## Slide Templates by Type

### Executive Summary Slide
```
Title: [Governing thought — one sentence recommendation]

SITUATION: [1 sentence of undisputed context]
COMPLICATION: [1–2 sentences on the tension or problem]
RECOMMENDATION: [1 sentence — restate governing thought]

KEY SUPPORTS:
• [Argument 1 in 10 words or fewer]
• [Argument 2 in 10 words or fewer]
• [Argument 3 in 10 words or fewer]

ASK: [One sentence — what you need from the audience]
```

### Argument Slide (most common)
```
Title: [Action title — the claim this slide proves]

• [Specific insight + data point]
• [Specific insight + data point]
• [Specific insight + data point]

[VISUAL: chart / stat callout / icon grid]
  → Annotation: what the visual proves

SPEAKER NOTE: This slide answers "[what question from the previous slide]".
The key thing to emphasize is [X]. Anticipated challenge: [Y].
```

### Data / Chart Slide
```
Title: [What the chart proves — not what it shows]

[CHART: line / bar / scatter / etc.]
  → Annotation on key data point: "[1 sentence explaining the spike/trend/gap]"
  → Benchmark line labeled: "[industry avg / target / prior year]"

SPEAKER NOTE: Don't read the chart. Say: "What this shows us is [X].
The reason this matters is [Y]. What we recommend doing about it is [Z]."
```

### Transition / Chapter Header Slide
```
Title: [The pillar / chapter name as a claim]
Subtitle: [1 sentence framing what this section covers]

[Optional: 3-box summary of what's in this chapter]
```

### Call to Action Slide
```
Title: [Specific ask — not "Next Steps"]

We need [specific decision / resource / commitment] by [date] to [achieve outcome].

NEXT STEPS:
• [Owner]: [Action] by [Date]
• [Owner]: [Action] by [Date]
• [Owner]: [Action] by [Date]
```

## Bullet Writing Rules

**Bad bullets (topics):**
```
• Comprehensive digital strategy review
• Important findings for leadership
• Multiple cost reduction opportunities
```

**Good bullets (insights):**
```
• Digital-only banks grew new accounts 3x faster than incumbents in 2024
• Our cost-to-serve is $38 vs. $22 for digital-native competitors — a 73% gap
• Three cost initiatives can close $85M of the $120M efficiency gap by FY26
```

Rule: Every bullet should be able to stand alone as a claim. If you removed the title, would the bullet still communicate something specific? If not, rewrite it.

## Speaker Notes Formula

For every slide, the speaker notes should answer:
1. **What question does this slide answer?** (from the previous slide)
2. **What is the ONE thing to emphasize?**
3. **What objection might come up, and how to handle it?**

Template:
```
This slide answers: "[question the audience has at this point]"
Emphasize: [key point or data]
If challenged on [X], respond: [brief framing]
Transition to next slide: "[connecting phrase that creates the next question]"
```

---

## Horizontal Logic Check

Before finalizing slide content, read the action titles of every slide in sequence — out loud.

Ask:
- Do the titles alone tell a coherent story?
- Does each title raise a question that the next slide answers?
- Does the governing thought appear by the 5th slide at the latest?
- Would a time-pressed executive understand the recommendation from the titles alone?

If the answer to any of these is no, fix the titles before touching the content.
