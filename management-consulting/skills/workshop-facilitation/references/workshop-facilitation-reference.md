## Facilitation Techniques

### Divergent (Idea Generation) Techniques

**Silent Brainstorming (Nominal Group Technique)**
Best for: Generating quantity of ideas, quieting dominant voices, ensuring equal participation

- Pose question: "What capabilities should future process have?" or "What are pain points in current workflow?"
- Silent generation: Everyone writes ideas on sticky notes (5-10 min)
- Round-robin sharing: Each person shares one idea, no discussion yet (10-15 min)
- Clustering: Group related ideas (10-15 min)
- Discussion: Explain/clarify grouped ideas (10-15 min)
- Voting: Dot voting on top priorities (5 min)

**Advantages:** Equal voice, high idea volume, introverts heard, reduces groupthink
**Disadvantages:** Less spontaneous debate/building, can feel mechanical

**Freewriting/Rapid Ideation**
Best for: Generating volume quickly, exploring without judgment, capturing raw thinking

- 15-minute rapid-fire brainstorm with no judgment or evaluation
- "What if we..." "How might we..." framing
- Capture everything on whiteboard or flip chart
- Cluster afterward
- Useful for culture/operating model discussions (less structured)

**Building/Stacking**
Best for: Collaborative ideation, building on others' ideas, team ownership

- One person presents idea
- Others add: "Yes, and..."
- Build toward integrated idea
- Useful for process workflows (design step by step)
- Creates ownership across team

### Convergent (Decision-Making) Techniques

**Dot Voting**
Best for: Quick prioritization, multiple items to rank, democratic weighting

- Display all ideas/options
- Each person gets 3-5 dots (sticky dots or marks)
- Can put multiple dots on one option or distribute
- Tally dots, rank items
- 15-minute process for prioritizing 30-50 items

**Affinity Mapping**
Best for: Finding patterns in ideas, grouping by theme, revealing structure

- Start with 50-100 ideas on cards/stickies
- Participants silently move cards into groups with no discussion
- When movement stops, label each group with a theme
- Name and describe each group
- Useful for: pain points, capabilities, success criteria

**Criteria Matrix / Decision Matrix**
Best for: Objective comparison of options against weighted criteria

Example: Evaluating 3 organizational structure options

| Criteria | Weight | Option A Score | Weighted A | Option B Score | Weighted B | Option C Score | Weighted C |
|----------|--------|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spans of control (lean) | 25% | 3/5 | 0.75 | 5/5 | 1.25 | 2/5 | 0.50 |
| Customer responsiveness | 25% | 4/5 | 1.00 | 3/5 | 0.75 | 4/5 | 1.00 |
| Cost efficiency | 20% | 5/5 | 1.00 | 2/5 | 0.40 | 4/5 | 0.80 |
| Talent retention | 20% | 3/5 | 0.60 | 4/5 | 0.80 | 2/5 | 0.40 |
| Adoption risk | 10% | 3/5 | 0.30 | 5/5 | 0.50 | 2/5 | 0.20 |
| **TOTAL** | 100% | | **3.65** | | **3.70** | | **2.90** |

Process:
1. Identify criteria (5-7 major decision factors)
2. Weight criteria (based on what matters most)
3. Score each option against each criterion (1-5 scale)
4. Calculate totals
5. Discuss surprising results; adjust scoring if warranted
6. Make decision based on analysis

**Six Thinking Hats (Edward de Bono)**
Best for: Structured debate, considering multiple perspectives, avoiding one-mindedness

- **White hat:** Facts and data only (what do we know?)
- **Red hat:** Intuition and feelings (gut reaction?)
- **Black hat:** Critical, identify risks and problems (what could go wrong?)
- **Yellow hat:** Optimistic, benefits, best case (what's the upside?)
- **Green hat:** Creative alternatives (how else could we approach this?)
- **Blue hat:** Process and control (are we making progress? what's our decision?)

Use: Have each group or each round analyze an option through one "hat." Ensures all perspectives considered.

### Participant Management Techniques

**Drawing Out Quiet Participants**
- Use round-robin: "Let's go around the table; each person shares one thought"
- Written input first: Silent brainstorming before group discussion
- Small group discussions: More intimate than full group
- 1:1 check-ins: "I noticed you haven't spoken; wanted to check if you have thoughts"
- "I'd like to hear from someone who hasn't spoken yet"

**Managing Dominant Voices**
- Gently interrupt: "Thanks John, let's make sure we hear from others too"
- Time-box speakers: "We have 2 minutes per person so everyone gets a turn"
- Use structured rounds: Silent brainstorm then round-robin, not free-for-all
- Group-generated ground rules: "One person talks at a time" agreed at start
- Parking lot: "That's a great point; let's capture it for the parking lot and stay focused on..."

**Handling Executives vs. Practitioners**
- Some executives want to decide; some want to understand options
- Clarify upfront: "We'll present options, gather input from the team, then make a decision"
- Give executives input opportunity early; they don't need to stay entire time
- Translate between worlds: Help executives understand practitioner reality; help practitioners understand strategic context
- Separate discussions if needed: Executives meet separately to align, then present aligned position to broader group

**Managing Conflict/Disagreement**
- Legitimate debate is healthy; get at root of disagreement
- "It sounds like you two are disagreeing on X. Help me understand why"
- Look for underlying interests, not just stated positions
- Can you reframe disagreement as a trade-off rather than right/wrong?
- If unresolvable, escalate to decision-maker: "This is a values trade-off; let's take this to the sponsor"

## Virtual Facilitation

### Virtual Workshop Setup

**Platform Selection:**
- **Miro, Mural, FigJam:** Visual collaboration, whiteboarding, spatial organization (best for design/brainstorm workshops)
- **Zoom + Mural:** Breakout rooms in Zoom, live collaboration in Mural (hybrid experience)
- **Teams + SharePoint:** Integrated with Microsoft ecosystem (if client locked in)

**Pre-Workshop Setup:**
- Test technology 30 min before start
- Send Zoom link + Mural/board link 24 hours before
- Brief: "This is interactive; bring your thinking; camera on preferred"
- Have IT support on standby

### Virtual Facilitation Techniques

**Engagement in Virtual Settings**
- Shorter segments: 45-60 min max before break (vs 75-90 in person)
- More frequent breaks: 10 min per hour of activity
- Camera on: Much more engaging than muted video off
- Reduce passive listening: Every 5-10 min, interactive element

**Breakout Rooms**
- Assign breakout groups deliberately (mix functions, include at least one confident voice)
- Give group clear task: "You have 15 min to design the future payment process. Use the Mural board; we'll report back."
- Assign reporter: "Sarah, you'll present your group's thinking; others add any points"
- Tight time boxes: People focus harder with clear time limit

**Silent Brainstorm in Mural/Miro**
- Pose question
- Everyone contributes to virtual board simultaneously (5-10 min silent)
- No live discussion; just building/adding
- Creates equal voice, less dominating
- Then group/cluster together in next phase

**Polling and Voting**
- Quick pulse: "Rate your confidence in this approach 1-5" (live polling in Zoom)
- Dot voting on Mural: Click to place votes on options
- Quick temperature check: "Thumbs up/down on this direction?"

### Virtual vs. In-Person Trade-offs

| Factor | In-Person | Virtual |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Engagement** | Higher energy, informal networking | Lower energy, requires more structure |
| **Participation** | Easier to read body language, include quiet voices | Harder to draw out quiet participants, easier to zone out |
| **Breakout Discussions** | Natural side conversations, fluid | Requires explicit breakout rooms, more structured |
| **Duration** | Can sustain 7-8 hours with energy management | Better at 4-5 hours, spread across days |
| **Creativity** | Whiteboard, physical movement, high interaction | Visual boards (Mural), good for certain activities |
| **Cost** | Travel, facilities | Lower cost, accessible to distributed teams |
| **Timezone** | Everyone in room | May require split sessions if global |

**Hybrid Recommendation:** In-person for design workshops (need flow, creativity, relationship building). Virtual works well for decision workshops (more structured) and discovery (can spread across days with smaller segments).

## Decision-Making in Workshops

### Consensus vs. Informed Captain

**Consensus Decision**
- Everyone agrees (or can live with) the decision
- Build agreement through discussion, adjustment, buy-in
- Slower but higher commitment
- Use when: Decision affects many functions, execution requires broad ownership

**Informed Captain (Leader Decision)**
- Leader consults team, considers input, then decides
- Faster, clear accountability
- Use when: Time-sensitive decision, or clear decision authority already held by executive

**Recommendation:**
- Be explicit upfront about who decides: "This workshop will inform John's (VP Finance) decision. We'll gather options and input; John will decide."
- Don't pretend consensus if it's really informed captain
- Teams know the difference; being clear builds trust

### Multi-Option Evaluation Process

**Step 1: Develop Options** (diverge)
- Generate 3-5 credible options
- Clearly articulate each option and what it entails
- Don't load option language (avoid "Option A: Risky; Option B: Safe")

**Step 2: Define Criteria** (converge)
- What matters in this decision? (cost, speed, risk, customer impact, feasibility, strategic fit, culture fit)
- Weight criteria (are all equally important?)
- Get agreement on criteria before scoring

**Step 3: Score Options Against Criteria** (converge)
- Use criteria matrix
- Score objectively
- Discuss any surprising results
- Adjust scores if discussion reveals new information

**Step 4: Discuss Trade-offs** (converge)
- "Option A is cheapest but takes longest. Option B costs more but faster. Which trade-off do we prefer?"
- Quantify trade-offs when possible
- Acknowledge what we're giving up with each choice

**Step 5: Decide** (close)
- Leader makes call
- Confirm buy-in: "Are we all comfortable moving forward with this approach?"
- Address remaining concerns: "I hear that concern. Here's how we'll manage it..."

## Pre-Work and Follow-Up

### Pre-Workshop Materials

**Send 2-3 Days Before:**
1. **Agenda:** Clear timeline, objectives, who should attend, bring what
2. **Pre-read:** 5-10 page background document (current state, business case, key questions)
3. **Pre-work assignment:** "Come with your top 3 pain points in current process" or "Review attached market data and come with top trends you see"
4. **Virtual details:** Zoom link, Mural board link, technical check-in instructions

**Benefits of Pre-Work:**
- Participants arrive with context, less time spent catching them up
- Homework activates thinking upfront; better quality contributions
- Shortens workshop by 30-45 minutes
- Creates shared baseline understanding

### Post-Workshop Synthesis and Distribution

**Within 24 Hours of Workshop:**
1. **Photo/capture:** All whiteboard content, sticky notes, outputs
2. **Summarize key decisions:** What did we decide?
3. **List action items:** Who's doing what by when?
4. **Identify parking lot items:** What didn't we resolve? When will we?

**Within 1 Week:**
1. **Distribute full readout** (3-5 pages):
   - Objectives and attendees
   - Key themes/insights
   - Decisions made
   - Action items with owners and dates
   - Next steps and timeline
2. **Share artifacts:** Photos of whiteboards, design sketches, options document
3. **Solicit feedback:** "What worked well? What should we improve next time?"

**Follow-Up Communication:**
- Send action item reminders: "Week 1, Week 3, Day before due date"
- Hold 30-min team meetings to validate synthesis (before sending to full org)
- Synthesize output into more polished deliverables: process map, design document, decision memo

## Common Workshop Pitfalls

| Pitfall | What It Looks Like | How to Avoid |
|---------|------------------|-------------|
| **Workshop as presentation** | Consultant presents for 6 hours; limited group discussion | Keep input to 40%; ensure 60% is group work and discussion |
| **Too many participants** | 25+ people; impossible to hear everyone | Split into smaller groups by function; reconvene to share |
| **No clear output defined** | People leave thinking "that was interesting but what happened?" | State upfront: "We will leave with a decision on X" or "We will have designed the future Y" |
| **No decision authority in room** | Group discusses but no one can decide; delays next steps | Ensure decision-maker is present and has allocated time |
| **Dominated by loudest voice** | Extroverts control conversation; introverts silent | Use silent brainstorm, round-robin, small group discussions |
| **Wrong participants** | Inviting peers when you need decision-makers, or including everyone when you need subject matter experts | Be intentional: Who needs to be here? Who can actually influence this? |
| **No time management** | First activity runs long; rest of agenda pushed | Use visible timer; honor time boxes; edit scope not time |
| **Unresolved conflict left hanging** | Two leaders disagree; facilitator ignores it | Address directly: "I see different views here. Let's talk through it" or escalate to sponsor |
| **No action items or accountability** | "That was great!" but no one knows what to do next | Capture actions with owner and date; send summary within 24 hours |
| **Virtual fatigue** | 6-hour virtual workshop with no breaks; people tuning out | Shorter segments (45 min max), frequent breaks (10 min/hour), interactive activities |

- Clustering/themes
- List of confirmed pain points with frequency (how many people mentioned)

## Accelerated Solutions Environment (ASE) Methodology

ASE is a collaborative, multi-day event format originally developed to bring diverse stakeholders together to co-create and align on solutions to complex business challenges. ASE workshops address "wicked problems" by stripping away rational, emotional, and political blockers through structured creative processes.

**Key ASE Principles:**
- Whole-person engagement (rational, emotional, political dimensions)
- Diverge-converge cycles (expand thinking, then focus)
- Every voice matters — democratic participation regardless of hierarchy
- The facilitator guides process, not content — participants own the outcomes
- Multi-sensory, high-energy environment — not a standard meeting
- Sponsor alignment before the event is critical

**When to Use ASE vs. Standard Workshop:**

| Factor | ASE Workshop | Standard Workshop |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| Problem complexity | High — "wicked problems" with no obvious answer | Moderate — defined scope, known solution space |
| Stakeholder diversity | Many functions, levels, and perspectives needed | Primarily single team or function |
| Duration | 2-5 days | Half-day to full day |
| Energy level | High — multi-sensory, movement, creative exercises | Moderate — structured discussion and analysis |
| Output | Co-created solutions with shared ownership | Decisions, plans, or analysis |
| Investment | High — significant preparation and facilitation | Moderate — standard meeting prep |

### ASE Pre-Workshop Design

#### Sharpening the Challenge

A good ASE challenge is complex, multi-stakeholder, and doesn't have an obvious answer.

**Challenge Statement Formula:**
"How might [stakeholder group] [achieve desired outcome] despite [key blocker], so that [business impact]?"

**Examples:**
- "How might our supply chain organization achieve 30% faster order fulfillment despite legacy system constraints, so that customer satisfaction scores reach 90%?"
- "How might the leadership team align on a digital transformation roadmap despite competing priorities across divisions, so that we achieve $50M in efficiency gains within 3 years?"

**Challenge Quality Test:**
- Is it genuinely complex? (If the answer is obvious, it's not an ASE problem)
- Are multiple stakeholder perspectives needed?
- Does it require both creative and analytical thinking?
- Is there organizational urgency?
- Will a multi-day investment be justified by the outcome?

#### Stakeholder Mapping for ASE

Recommend a diverse mix for the room:

| Role | Why They're Needed | Ideal # |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| Decision-makers | Can commit resources and approve direction | 2-4 |
| Subject matter experts | Bring deep technical or functional knowledge | 5-8 |
| Frontline operators | Know what actually works (and doesn't) | 3-5 |
| Cross-functional representatives | Bring adjacent perspectives and dependencies | 3-5 |
| Provocateurs/outsiders | Challenge assumptions, bring external viewpoints | 1-3 |
| Facilitators | Guide process, manage energy, synthesize | 2-3 |

**Ideal group size:** 20-40 participants. Below 15 limits diversity; above 50 makes facilitation very difficult.

#### Sponsor Alignment

Generate a sponsor briefing covering:
- Workshop objectives and desired outcomes
- Success criteria — what does "good" look like at the end?
- Non-negotiables — decisions that are off the table
- Expected outputs — what deliverables will come from this?
- Post-event commitments — sponsor's role in maintaining momentum
- Budget and logistics — venue, travel, materials, catering
- Communication plan — who needs to know, before and after

#### ASE Pre-Read & Pre-Work Package

Send to participants before the workshop:

**Context-Setting Document (send 7-10 days before):**
- The challenge statement and why it matters now
- What's been tried before (and why it didn't fully work)
- Key data points and market context
- Stakeholder perspectives summary

**Provocative Data or Trend Briefing (send 5-7 days before):**
- 3-5 challenging data points that reframe the problem
- External benchmarks or competitor moves
- Customer/market signals that create urgency

**Personal Reflection Questions (send 3-5 days before):**
- "What would you do if failure wasn't an option?"
- "What's the one thing we're not talking about that we should be?"
- "If you could change one thing about how we work today, what would it be?"

**Logistics & Expectations (send 3-5 days before):**
- Schedule, venue, dress code
- "This is not a normal meeting" — set expectations for energy level, participation, creative exercises
- Any pre-work assignments to complete

### ASE Icebreaker Pool

Select based on group size, familiarity, and energy needed:

| Icebreaker | Time | Group Size | Energy | Best For |
|-----------|------|------------|--------|----------|
| Personal Object Story | 5-10 min | < 20 | Medium | Building personal connection |
| Two Truths & a Wish | 10 min | Any | Medium | Mixing fun with challenge context |
| Speed Networking Bingo | 10-15 min | 15+ | High | Large groups, getting people moving |
| Headline from the Future | 10 min | Any | Medium-High | Setting aspirational tone |
| Marshmallow Challenge | 15-20 min | Any | High | Multi-day opener, team building |
| Gallery Walk Introductions | 20 min | < 25 | Medium | Deep introductions, visual thinkers |

**Descriptions:**
- **Personal Object Story**: Each participant brings an object that represents their approach to problem-solving; share in pairs then brief plenary
- **Two Truths & a Wish**: State two truths and one thing you wish were true about the challenge — group guesses which is the wish
- **Speed Networking Bingo**: Bingo card with challenge-related prompts ("Find someone who has worked on this problem for 5+ years")
- **Headline from the Future**: Write the newspaper headline you'd want to see in 2 years about this challenge
- **Marshmallow Challenge**: Teams build the tallest freestanding structure using spaghetti, tape, string, and a marshmallow
- **Gallery Walk Introductions**: Each participant creates a visual poster about themselves and their perspective; gallery walk to view

### ASE Diverge-Converge Work Blocks

Structure core work as diverge-converge cycles:

**Diverge Phase (Expand Thinking):**

| Activity | Duration | Format | Purpose |
|----------|----------|--------|---------|
| Individual reflection | 5-10 min | Silent writing/thinking | Generate personal ideas before group influence |
| Pair/trio work | 10-20 min | Small conversation | Build on individual ideas, develop themes |
| Small group work | 30-60 min | 4-8 people | Deep collaborative exploration of sub-challenges |

**Small Group Frameworks:**
- **Affinity mapping** — clustering ideas into themes
- **"How Might We..." reframing** — turning problems into opportunity statements
- **Dot voting** — democratic prioritization
- **Prototype/sketch solutions** — quick visual concepts
- **Pre-mortem analysis** — "imagine this failed — why?"

**Converge Phase (Focus Thinking):**

| Activity | Duration | Format | Purpose |
|----------|----------|--------|---------|
| Plenary share-back | 15-30 min | Group presentations | Cross-pollinate ideas across teams |
| Whole-room synthesis | 20-40 min | Facilitated discussion | Converge on key decisions and commitments |
| Decision point | 10-20 min | Voting or leader decision | Lock in direction |

### ASE Timing Guidelines

- Never run a work block longer than 60 minutes without a break or energizer
- Schedule energizers at: post-lunch, mid-afternoon, and any time energy visibly drops
- For multi-day events: Day 1 = diverge (explore broadly), Day 2 = converge (focus on solutions and commitments)
- Buffer 10-15% of total time for overruns and spontaneous discussions
- Lunch should be at least 45 minutes — it's networking time, not wasted time
- Morning = high-energy, creative, divergent work
- Afternoon = structured, analytical, convergent work

### ASE Post-Workshop Follow-Through

**Within 24 hours:**
- Thank-you message to all participants
- 3 key decisions/commitments made (one-pager)
- Photos of key artifacts shared via email or collaboration platform

**Within 3 days:**
Full workshop output document including:
- Photos of all artifacts (sticky notes, whiteboards, posters)
- Synthesized key themes and decisions
- Action items with owners and deadlines
- Parking lot items (ideas captured but not addressed)
- Participant feedback summary

**Within 1 week:**
Sponsor debrief covering:
- What worked well in the workshop
- What to adjust for future sessions
- How to maintain momentum
- Any political or organizational issues surfaced

**Within 2-4 weeks:**
Follow-up pulse check:
- Are action items progressing?
- Have any blockers emerged?
- Does the group need a "booster session"?
- Updated action tracker with status

**Optional:**
Create a short video or visual summary for broader stakeholder communication.

**Key Principles for Effective Workshops:**
- **Clear purpose:** Everyone knows what you're trying to achieve
- **Diverse participation:** The best ideas come from cross-functional input
- **Structured but flexible:** Have an agenda but adapt if discussions reveal new angles
- **Psychological safety:** People speak up when they feel safe, when they know they won't be judged
- **Balance input and output:** Aim for 40% expert input, 60% group work
- **Energy management:** Design for peak cognitive load in morning; synthesis in afternoon
- **Closing strong:** Clear decisions, clear next steps, clear accountability
- **Follow through:** Workshop is step 1; synthesis, validation, and action delivery are steps 2-4
