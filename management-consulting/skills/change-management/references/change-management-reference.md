## Impact Assessment

### Human Impact Analysis

For each stakeholder group, assess:

| Impact Area | Questions | Assessment |
|------------|-----------|------------|
| **Role & Responsibilities** | What will their job description change? New activities? Deleted activities? | High/Medium/Low impact |
| **Skills & Knowledge** | What new skills are required? What knowledge becomes obsolete? | High/Medium/Low impact |
| **Systems & Tools** | What systems will they use? What's new, retired, or replaced? | High/Medium/Low impact |
| **Reporting Structure** | Will reporting lines change? New managers? Matrix relationships? | High/Medium/Low impact |
| **Compensation & Incentives** | Do performance metrics change? Bonus drivers? Career paths? | High/Medium/Low impact |
| **Physical Environment** | Office location? Remote vs. co-located? Office design? | High/Medium/Low impact |
| **Team Dynamics** | New colleagues? Team consolidation? New team composition? | High/Medium/Low impact |

**High-impact areas require investment in communication, training, and support.**

### Process & Technology Impact

- **Process changes:** Document current workflows → document future workflows → identify gaps → design training
- **System changes:** What data fields change? Workflows within systems? Integrations? New systems?
- **Technology adoption:** Technical training requirements, change to way of working, system capacity/performance

## Communication Planning

### Communication Strategy Template

**Objectives:** What mindsets/behaviors do we need to shift?
- Awareness: People understand what's changing and why
- Understanding: People know how it affects them specifically
- Acceptance: People agree this is the right direction
- Adoption: People actively use/follow new processes

**Audience Segmentation:** Who needs different messages?
- By role: executives, managers, practitioners, support functions
- By geography: headquarters vs. field locations
- By adoption readiness: champions, early adopters, mainstream, laggards

**Key Messages:**
- **What?** The specific change (process, system, structure, capability)
- **Why?** Business rationale (market pressure, efficiency, capability, customer value)
- **When?** Timeline and milestones
- **How?** People's role in making it happen, what they need to do
- **What's in it for me (WIIFM)?** How does it benefit them or the organization they care about?

**Communication Channels:**
| Channel | Best For | Frequency | Owner |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| All-hands meetings | Broad announcements, CEO visibility, questions | Monthly | Sponsorship |
| Manager briefings | Context for managers, handling objections | Bi-weekly | Change lead |
| Email/newsletter | Updates, reference material, deadline reminders | Bi-weekly | Communications |
| Intranet/SharePoint | FAQs, training materials, progress updates | Ongoing | Communications |
| Lunch & learns | Training, demos, peer learning | Weekly | Training team |
| Steering committee | Decision-making, escalations, governance | Bi-weekly | Program leadership |
| 1:1 coaching | Personalized support for high-impact individuals | On-demand | Change agents |

**Sample Monthly Communication Cadence:**
- Week 1: Manager briefing (give managers talking points)
- Week 1: All-hands Q&A or town hall
- Week 2: Targeted communications to key groups (finance team, operations team, etc.)
- Week 3: Progress update + training schedule announcement
- Week 4: Spotlight success stories, celebrate quick wins

### Message Framework by Audience

**For Executives/Sponsors:**
- Business impact and strategic alignment
- Risk and mitigation
- Progress against plan
- Executive decisions required

**For Managers:**
- How their team's work will change
- What support they need to lead their teams
- Timeline and key dates
- Escalation process

**For Individual Contributors:**
- How their job will change specifically
- What training/support is available
- What's expected of them
- When and where to get help

**For Support Functions (IT, HR, Finance):**
- Dependencies on their work
- Timeline implications
- Resource needs
- Success metrics

### Escalation Protocol

Define clear escalation paths for:
- Misunderstanding or confusion → manager → change lead → sponsorship
- Resistance or concerns → manager → change lead → sponsorship
- Operational blockers → business owner → change lead → steering committee
- Urgent issues → change lead → sponsor

## Training Design

### Training Needs Assessment

| Question | How to Assess |
|----------|---------------|
| What new knowledge/skills are required? | Compare current job description + current skill profile to future state |
| How many people need training? | Stakeholder map, organizational chart |
| What's their starting point? | Current system experience, literacy level, learning preferences |
| What's the urgency? | Go-live date, parallel run period, cutover approach |
| What's the learning environment? | In-person classroom, virtual, blended, self-paced, on-the-job |

### Training Curriculum Design

**Course 1: Organizational Overview (30-45 min)**
- Why the change is happening (business case)
- How it affects the organization
- Timeline and rollout approach
- Where to get help

**Course 2: Process Overview for Your Role (60-90 min, role-specific)**
- Current vs. future workflows (side-by-side comparison)
- Your specific tasks in future state
- Key changes that affect you
- Common questions

**Course 3: System Navigation (60-120 min, system-specific)**
- System overview and structure
- Key navigation paths
- Step-by-step guide for critical transactions
- Common mistakes and how to fix them

**Course 4: Advanced Topics (30-60 min, optional)**
- Exception handling
- Reporting and analytics
- Optimization techniques
- Workarounds for edge cases

**Course 5: Reinforcement & Coaching (ongoing)**
- Just-in-time support
- Help desk escalation
- Peer coaching
- 1:1 coaching for struggling learners

### Delivery Methods

| Method | Best For | Pros | Cons |
|--------|----------|------|------|
| **Instructor-led classroom** | Complex content, hands-on practice, high interaction | Engaging, immediate Q&A, practice in group | Scheduling, travel, scale limitations |
| **Virtual instructor-led** | Distributed teams, remote-first | Scalable, accessible, recorded | Less interactive, technology dependent, fatigue |
| **Self-paced e-learning** | Asynchronous, reference material, scale | Flexible, cost-effective, self-directed | Less engaging, high dropout, harder for complex topics |
| **Hands-on labs** | System/tool training, decision-making | Builds confidence, practical, retention | Resource-intensive, needs environment setup |
| **Job aids/quick guides** | Reference, just-in-time support | Always available, searchable, low cost | Not for initial learning, limited depth |
| **Peer coaching** | One-on-one support, role-specific nuance | Personalized, credible, high retention | Inconsistent quality, hard to scale |
| **Super-user program** | Building internal capability, embedding change agents | Sustainable, credible, ownership | Training time-intensive |

### Training Effectiveness Measurement

Measure at each Kirkpatrick level:

**Level 1 - Reaction:** Did people find it useful?
- Post-training survey: clarity, relevance, trainer effectiveness, pace
- Rating scale and open feedback

**Level 2 - Learning:** Did they learn?
- Knowledge checks/quizzes
- System navigation proficiency assessments
- Post-training test scores

**Level 3 - Behavior:** Are they applying it?
- System transaction audit (are people using new process correctly?)
- Manager assessment of team capability
- Help desk ticket volume and type (decreasing issues = learning stuck)
- Observe in real workflows

**Level 4 - Results:** Is organizational outcome improving?
- Process cycle time
- Error rates
- Customer satisfaction
- Adoption rate (% of target population using new process)

**Recommended measurement cadence:**
- Week 1: Level 1 (reaction)
- Week 2-3: Level 2 (learning via assessments) + Level 3 (behavior observation)
- Month 1-2: Level 3 (ongoing behavior observation)
- Month 2-3: Level 4 (business metrics trending)

## Resistance Management

### Root Causes of Resistance

| Root Cause | Symptoms | Intervention |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Lack of awareness** | "I didn't know this was changing" | Increase communication frequency and channels |
| **Misunderstanding** | "I don't think this will work" | Clarify, provide evidence, involve in design |
| **Lack of skills** | "I don't know how to do this" | Provide training, coaching, job aids |
| **Fear of loss** | "I'm worried about my job" | Provide security/role clarity, involve in transition planning |
| **Competing priorities** | "I don't have time for this" | Reduce workload, provide resources, adjust timeline |
| **Disagreement with direction** | "I think this is the wrong approach" | Listen, involve in design, acknowledge concerns |
| **Past change fatigue** | "We always do this and nothing sticks" | Show commitment, allocate resources, demonstrate success |
| **Organizational misalignment** | "Management isn't serious about this" | Ensure visible sponsorship, align incentives, model behaviors |

### Resistance Intervention Playbook

**Level 1 - Communication & Education**
- Clarify the change message
- Provide evidence and business case
- Share success stories from other organizations
- Address FAQs publicly

**Level 2 - Involvement & Co-Design**
- Invite resistors into design process
- Give them voice in approach
- Show how their input shaped solution
- Often their concerns are valid; addressing them strengthens the change

**Level 3 - Targeted Coaching & Support**
- 1:1 conversations with key resistors
- Understand their specific concerns
- Problem-solve together
- Provide extra training or support

**Level 4 - Escalation & Accountability**
- If resistant behavior is derailing change, escalate to manager
- Set expectations for participation
- Document non-compliance
- Last resort: manage out (rare, but needed if someone consistently undermines)

### Escalation Protocol for Resistance

1. **Identify:** Who is resisting? What specifically are they resisting?
2. **Understand:** Why? What's driving their resistance?
3. **Engage:** Have a conversation. Listen first.
4. **Intervene:** Use appropriate strategy based on root cause
5. **Monitor:** Is approach working? Adjust if needed.
6. **Escalate:** If individual is creating organizational risk, involve their manager

## Adoption Tracking

### Adoption Metrics

**Awareness:**
- % of target population who can articulate what's changing
- Measured via: survey, pulse check, conversation sampling

**Understanding:**
- % who understand how it affects their specific role
- Measured via: survey, knowledge assessment, manager feedback

**Adoption:**
- % actively using/following new process
- Measured via: system transaction data, process audit, manager assessment
- Target: 80%+ by end of first month, 95%+ by month 3

**Proficiency:**
- % performing new tasks with accuracy and efficiency
- Measured via: transaction error rates, cycle time, quality metrics
- Target: Within 10% of experienced users by month 2-3

**Sustainability:**
- Change remains stable over time, no backsliding
- Measured via: ongoing system/process metrics, audit trails

### Adoption Dashboard

Track weekly/monthly:

| Metric | Target | Week 1 | Week 2 | Week 3 | Week 4 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Training completed (% of target) | 100% | 60% | 80% | 95% | 100% |
| System logins (% of target pop) | 100% | 45% | 70% | 85% | 95% |
| Transactions completed correctly (accuracy) | 95% | 75% | 85% | 90% | 94% |
| Help desk tickets (volume, trending down) | Baseline | 450 | 380 | 280 | 150 |
| Manager assessment: team capability | 4+/5 | 2.5 | 3.0 | 3.5 | 4.2 |

### Intervention Triggers

If any metric falls below target by >15%:
1. Investigate: What's driving the gap?
2. Intervene: Targeted support, additional training, communication
3. Monitor: Does intervention close the gap?
4. Escalate: If gap persists, escalate to sponsorship

## Readiness Assessment

### Pre-Implementation Readiness Checklist

**Sponsorship (Must-Haves)**
- [ ] Executive sponsor assigned and committed
- [ ] Sponsor has allocated time/attention
- [ ] Change leadership team identified and briefed
- [ ] Steering committee established with clear governance
- [ ] Sponsor willing to have difficult conversations with resistors

**Organization (Must-Haves)**
- [ ] Capacity identified; workload rebalanced if needed
- [ ] Budget secured for training, change resources, communications
- [ ] Affected teams informed of timeline and expectations
- [ ] No major competing initiatives in parallel (or dependencies mapped)

**Change Readiness (Nice-to-Haves)**
- [ ] Organization has successfully managed similar changes
- [ ] Leadership team alignment on future state
- [ ] Communication infrastructure exists
- [ ] Training capability available
- [ ] Change team chartered and resourced

**Go/No-Go Decision**

**GO if:**
- Sponsorship is strong and committed
- Organization has capacity to execute
- Timeline is realistic
- Leadership is aligned

**Conditional/Phased if:**
- Minor gaps in sponsorship or capacity (mitigate with resources)
- Competing initiatives can be sequenced
- Timeline has flexibility

**NO-GO if:**
- Sponsorship is weak or absent
- Organization lacks capacity and can't be rebalanced
- Timeline is unrealistic
- Major conflicting initiatives with hard deadlines

**Key Principles for Change Management:**
- **Start early:** Change management begins at project inception, not at go-live
- **Involve stakeholders:** The people affected by change should shape it
- **Be transparent:** Share the business case, timeline, and risks openly
- **Tailor approaches:** Different stakeholders need different messages and support
- **Measure and adjust:** Track adoption; intervene quickly when metrics slip
- **Sustain the change:** Embedding new ways of working requires ongoing reinforcement, not just training
